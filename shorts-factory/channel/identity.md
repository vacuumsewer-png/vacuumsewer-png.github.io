## 1. Name and handle

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Channel name | **Predixel** |
| Handle | **@predixel** |
| Backup handle 1 | @predixelsims |
| Backup handle 2 | @predixellab |
| Tagline | You predict. The code decides. |

Why this name: "predict" + "pixel". The *predi-* root reads the same in English, Spanish, Portuguese,
French, Italian and is close in German and the Slavic languages, so the name hints at the format
(you make a prediction) without needing a translation. Eight letters, three syllables, one obvious
pronunciation (pre-DIK-sel). It does not name a mechanic, so it still fits if the channel adds
puzzles or math visuals.

Claim the handle the moment the channel exists. A 404 only shows that no channel answers at that
address today; YouTube can still refuse a handle (reserved, recently released, or judged too close
to a large channel), and that was not tested.

## 3. Channel description (paste as is)

```
Predixel makes short physics and logic simulations you can predict. Each video asks one simple question (which color wins? which path is fastest? what happens next?) and then lets the rules play out. Make your call before the end and see if you were right.

Every video on this channel is an original simulation written from scratch in code (Python). Every frame is computed and drawn by our own programs, and every sound is synthesized by the same code from what happens in the simulation. No talking and almost no text, so it works in any language.

Tell us your prediction in the comments, and tell us what to simulate next.
```

## 7. Visual identity

The whole identity is one idea: **coral is the ball and the path it has already taken (solid);
teal is where you think it will go (dotted)**. The avatar is a bold letter p whose bowl is that
path: an off-white stem and arc, a coral ball where the arc stops, and three shrinking teal dots
closing the letter. On the banner the wordmark is lowercase "predixel"; a coral trail bounces in
along a ruled floor line, hops across the tops of the letters and comes to rest as the dot of the
"i", and a teal dotted prediction continues over the last letters to a hollow ring marked "?".
Palette: Ink `#0A0E1F` (background), Navy `#182048` (the lit part of the background), Paper
`#F5F2EA` (letters and mark), Coral `#FF5C4D` (the ball, the past), Teal `#2FE0C8` (the
prediction), Amber `#FFC247` (rare highlight, for thumbnails and end cards only), Slate `#7C86A8`
(grid dots, ruler, quiet text). Fonts: Inter Display ExtraBold for the wordmark, tightly tracked
and lowercase; JetBrains Mono Medium, uppercase and widely tracked, for the tagline and any small
labels. Texture: a faint graph-paper dot grid that fades toward the edges. Rules: dark background
always; never more than coral plus teal as accents in one frame of brand material; the ball is
the only object with shading.

## 9. Honest weaknesses and unknowns

- Handle availability rests on HTTP 404 only. Not proven until the handle is actually claimed.
- No trademark, app-store or domain check was possible. One web search and the YouTube searches found no "Predixel".
- "Predixel" can be misheard as "Prediksel" or misread as "Predixle"; `@predixal` is already someone else's handle.
- "Pixel" may suggest pixel art. The simulations are smooth vector drawings.
- The tagline is English. On a phone the banner tagline is roughly 10 px tall, so treat it as decoration; the wordmark carries the banner.
- Below about 32 px the teal dots of the avatar disappear and it is a p with a coral ball. Letter-p avatars are common; the ball is what makes this one ours.
- The avatar background is dark, so on YouTube's dark theme the circle edge is quiet. Deliberate (matches the videos), but less loud than a bright disc.
- The watermark was checked on flat colors, not on real video frames.
- The video generator was still being built when this was made. Its scene colors were not matched; if end cards or thumbnails carry the brand, use the palette above.
- Category, language, remix, tag and keyword choices are heuristics. Nothing here was A/B tested.
