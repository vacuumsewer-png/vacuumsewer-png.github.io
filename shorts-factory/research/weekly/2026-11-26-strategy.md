YouTube now tests each new Short with fewer people (100–230 on day 1, down from ~1,000 a week ago) because our recent Shorts lose viewers fast.

### Why the hits worked and the flops did not (first frames side by side)

The two hits are Domino Giant (61% stayed) and Tipping Point (55%). In both:
- **Big, familiar, physical objects** (dominoes, weights on a beam) fill the frame.
- **They are already moving** in the first frame.
- **Little interface on screen.**

The flops are Bridge Load, Strike, Star Forge and Unshatter. In all of them:
- **Small abstract shapes** (dots, thin towers, beams).
- **Lots of interface:** answer chips A–D, rule lines, bars, and the "PICK NOW 3-2-1" panel that I added on 22 Nov. That panel covers the action in the first 3 seconds, which is exactly when viewers decide to stay.
- **Rules you have to read** before the video makes sense.

**Conclusion:** viewers stay when they understand the video in one glance with the sound off, and they love seeing something familiar grow to an absurd scale. They do not stay for clever rules, multiple-choice bands or abstract colored dots.

### What the market says (tracker: 52 channels, ~1,200 Shorts; plus web research)

**Winners repeat one format daily, with variations:**

| Channel | Format | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Bounce Theory | "X & Y Bowling Balls Shatter", posted every day | 1.8M views in 20 h on its latest |
| Memeland | "Will it reach the Final Form?" every day | 1–1.6M per video |
| Grid Loops | wooden tables flipping into holes | 2–5M |

The algorithm learns who likes a channel's format and keeps feeding it to them. My rule "every video a completely new mechanic" is the opposite of what works.

**Other findings:**
- **Real, tangible objects and textures** (metal, wood, glass) beat flat abstract shapes. 3D loop channels have a 246K median per Short; plain 2D sims have 24K.
- **Volume buys hits.** Channels under 1K subscribers that posted 52+ videos a year reached 1K at 30.8%, against 1.9% for 1–11 videos (vidIQ). Shorts-only channels hit 1K at a median of ~90 uploads. We have 19.
- **Realistic bar:** about 500 Shorts views per new subscriber. So 1,000 subscribers needs about 0.5M views, which means several Domino-size hits or one big one.

### Honest outlook

On the current track, the Partner Program (1,000 subscribers plus 10M engaged views in 90 days, applied for before 31 Jan 2027) **will not happen**. With the changes below, 1,000 subscribers by mid-January is possible but not certain. 10M engaged views needs at least one million-view breakout. I will report against milestones, not hopes.

## Decisions (effective now)

1. **One main format family, posted daily: "SCALE" (60% of uploads).**
   - **Structure:** familiar real objects (coins, bricks, cars, balloons, ice cubes, paper, toy blocks, water drops; dominoes are retired) grow or multiply until they reach something absurd (a skyscraper, a mountain, the Moon).
   - **Every video carries:** one big counter and the reference objects that make the scale real. The answer comes in the last 2–3 s.
   - **Title template:** "Each [object] Is [N]x Bigger… How Many to [goal]?" / "How Many [objects] Until [absurd outcome]?"
   - **Variety rule:** every episode uses a different object, goal and physics (stack, fill, lift, tip, flood). Never dominoes again, and never the same object twice in a row. This keeps the series a family, not a copy.

2. **A second family: "CRUSH" (25%).**
   - **What it is:** a press, wrecking ball or drop hits one familiar object (glass block, car, ice, watermelon-like ball) that breaks into pieces with a big count, as in OddlyCoded's 2.9M Short.
   - **Look:** glossy, shaded pieces.
   - **Title template:** "The Press Crushed One [object]… Into How Many Pieces?"

3. **Experiments (15%):** merge to final form, proliferation and the other lanes from 24 Nov, one at a time. Kill any experiment under 30% stayed or 1K views. **Stop now:** color races, A–D band quizzes and "what was it" puzzles.

4. **Visual rules for every Short:**
   - **Understood with sound off in 1 second.**
   - **The main object is large**, at least 25% of the frame height, **and already moving in frame 0.**
   - **On screen:** one question line (no more than 8 words) plus one counter. Nothing else: no rule lines, answer chips, bars, fine print, or "PICK NOW" panel.
   - **Pseudo-3D look in our 2D engine:** gradients, specular highlights, soft shadows, outlines and material colors (metal, wood, glass) instead of flat dots.
   - **Keep the escalation arc** (tension_bed sound, scale and stakes rising).

5. **Volume:** 2 Shorts a day, every day, once the SCALE and CRUSH templates exist. Templates make each episode cheap: a new object, numbers and reference art, the same proven engine.

6. **Fast feedback (every daily run checks Studio):**
   - **Double down:** any Short above 20K views within 48 h gets 3 more episodes in its family within 72 h.
   - **Fix:** any Short under 25% stayed means the next build in that family fixes the first 3 seconds.
   - **Kill:** a family with 5 Shorts in a row under 1.5K views gets killed.

7. **Comments:** the pinned comment asks for one number: "Comment your number 👇". The video's question invites a number too.

8. **3D pilot (needs the owner's OK to download Blender, free, from blender.org):**
   - **Hardware:** this PC has an RTX 3050 (4 GB) and 16 GB of RAM, so Blender EEVEE is realistic. CPU-only would not be.
   - **Pilot:** one headless test (`blender -b -P`) of a 3D CRUSH Short, rendered overnight.
   - **Adopt or drop:** adopt it if a full Short renders in under ~6 h and looks clearly better than 2D.
