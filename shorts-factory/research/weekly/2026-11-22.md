## 3. Where viewers stay and where they leave (retention curves)

The same shape repeats on every Short (values are % of starting viewers; above 100% means rewatches):

1. **0–3 s: strong.** The hook works, and curves start at 120–145%, so people loop and rewatch.
2. **3–9 s: the big leak.** Retention falls about 40 points in six seconds (Territory 114→69, Pressure Pie 114→69, Maze Flood 114→69, Event Horizon 119→77). This is the "setup" stretch, where contestants move but nothing has happened yet.
3. **9–22 s: flat.** People who survive the setup stay to the end (curve only drifts down 10–15 points).
4. **Last 2–3 s: a second drop.** After the winner is clear, viewers swipe before the loop restarts (Trail Trap 77→47, Fuse Race 71→37, Tipping Point 61→43).

Tipping Point keeps people longer because its question stays open until about 22 s. Fuse Race and Trail Trap have the best middles (75–85% held).

## 4. Why people don't subscribe

- Nothing on screen or in the end gives a reason to subscribe or come back. Each Short is a one-off.
- Viewers can't take part: the prediction is only in their head, so there is no comment, no score and no follow-up.
- 1 comment per video (ours). With no comments, YouTube has no signal that viewers care.

## 5. Action plan (starts with the next builds)

### A. Fix the 3–9 s leak (biggest win, all new Shorts)
- Something must happen by **2.5 s**: first crash, first elimination, or first lead change. Choose seeds where an early event happens (add it to each scene's drama score).
- Add an on-screen **"PICK NOW" countdown (3…2…1)** in the first 3 s, then "LOCKED IN". It turns the setup into a game moment.
- Keep Shorts at the short end of the range (22–26 s). Long setups lose viewers.

### B. More of what works: question-first formats
- Make at least **half the next builds number quizzes or one-question physics** like Domino Giant and Tipping Point ("How many…?", "Which falls first?", "Will it make it?").
- **Domino Giant 2** next (a different rule: 2x bigger, or upward stairs), while the original is still being pushed.
- Fewer plain color races. When there is a race, give it a twist or a number in the title.

### C. Make viewers take part (comments, subscriptions)
1. **"Comment your pick" on screen** during the countdown, with the same line as the first line of the description. Comments are a strong signal to the feed.
2. **"Predixel Score" streak:** the end card reads "Got it right? +1 to your streak. Subscribe to keep it." This gives a reason to subscribe tied to the game, not a generic "please subscribe".
3. **Viewers choose the next contest:** the pinned comment asks "Which color should get a rematch?" or "Next: 4 or 6 runners?" and the most-liked reply decides the next build. The next Short's description credits it ("You picked this one").
4. **Reply to and heart comments** every day (the pin-comments task does this): reply "Correct ✅" or "So close!" to guesses. Replies make more people comment.
5. **Community posts** (now unlocked): one poll every 2 days, such as "Which side falls first?" with the answer in the next Short. Polls reach subscribers and people who watched before.
6. **Rematch series:** some Shorts end with "REMATCH TOMORROW" (the losers come back with one change). This gives viewers a reason to subscribe and follow the series.

### D. Stop the end-of-video drop
- Show the winner later, in the last 1.5 s, and cut straight back to frame 0 so the loop feels like a replay.
- Put the score/streak line at the same moment as the reveal, not after it.

### E. Packaging and reach
- Titles: name a number or a surprising rule ("1.5x bigger", "only 2 tiles"). The two best titles both ask a concrete question.
- Keep ChatGPT thumbnails and the spoiler check. Thumbnails matter little for the Shorts feed but help in search and on the channel page.
- Group the Shorts in playlists by type ("Physics Quizzes", "Color Wars", "Races") so channel visits turn into binge watching.
- Keep posting at 12:00 and 19:00 ET to favor US viewers.

### F. Long videos (after a few hundred subscribers)
- Weekly 8–10 minute "Prediction Tournament": 12 rounds, an on-screen scoreboard and "pause and pick" moments, built from the same engines. Watch hours from these also count toward the 4,000-hour path.
