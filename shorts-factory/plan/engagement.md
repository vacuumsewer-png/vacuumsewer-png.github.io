# Engagement playbook: non-verbal prediction simulations, worldwide audience

Status 2026-11-16. Covers comments, replies, posts, on-video devices, daily Studio reading and policy risks.
Labels: **[H]** = heuristic (our working rule, not a YouTube fact). **[U]** = unknown or unverified; check in Studio before relying on it.
Prerequisite: comments must be on. Videos marked "made for kids" have comments disabled, so the audience setting (the owner's legal call, and it must be honest) decides whether sections 1 to 3 are possible at all.

## 1. Pinned comment on every video

Formula: **one question about the outcome + the options as emoji or numbers + "before the end"**. Nothing else.
- Post it from the channel account and pin it right after publishing, before most viewers arrive. [H]
- 15 words at most. Present tense, digits instead of number words, no idioms, no slang, no jokes that need a shared culture.
- The options match what is on screen (same colors, same numbers), so a viewer can answer with one emoji.
- Never reveal the result. No links. No "subscribe" or "like".
- Write a new one for each video. The same pinned text everywhere reads as a template to viewers and to reviewers. [H]
- If the video came from a viewer idea, the pinned comment also carries the credit (section 3).

Ready examples (swap in the options shown in the video):
1. Which color wins? 🔴 🔵 🟢 🟡 Write your pick before the end.
2. How many balls are left at the end? Guess a number first.
3. Left or right? ⬅️ ➡️ Pick one, then watch.
4. Will it get out? 👍 yes or 👎 no. Answer before the end.
5. Which one stops first: 1, 2 or 3?
6. How many seconds until the last one falls? Guess, then count.
7. Big and slow, or small and fast? ⚫ or ⚪ Who wins?
8. Same start, one small change. Is the result the same? Yes or no?
9. Who is the last one alive? 🟥 🟦 🟩 🟨
10. I guessed 🔵 and I was wrong. What was your first guess? (Use only when it is true.)

## 2. Reply rules

A written reply goes to, in this order:
1. Questions (how it is made, what the sound is, what the rules are).
2. Suggestions for a new mechanic or rule.
3. Predictions that give a reason, and exact bug reports ("blue went through the wall at 0:12").
4. A complaint that names something concrete.

Heart only: one-word picks, emoji, "first", repeats of a point already answered. No heart and no reply: insults, spam, fights between viewers.

Speed, counted from publishing [H]:
- Hours 0 to 2: one pass. Every real comment gets a reply or a heart.
- Hours 2 to 24: two more passes. Hours 24 to 48: one pass. After 48 hours: one weekly sweep with the "have not responded" filter in the Studio comments inbox.
- YouTube does not document that fast replies improve distribution [U]. The reasons are plainer: the commenter is notified and often returns, and new viewers see a channel that answers.

Tone and length:
- One line, about 15 words at most; "how is it made" may take two lines. Simple English, one emoji at most, no sarcasm (it does not translate).
- Be specific: name the color, the second, the number. Ask one follow-up question at most, and only if the answer helps a future video.
- Never argue. Give one calm factual answer, then stop, even if they answer back. For abuse: "Hide user from channel", no reply.
- Never ask for subscriptions, likes or shares in a reply. No links. No promised dates.
- Vary the wording. The templates are drafts: change the detail in { } every time and never paste one sentence many times in a row.
- Tell the truth about how the videos are made, including that an AI assistant writes code.
- Approve real comments that were held for review every day, so honest viewers are not silenced.

| # | Comment type | Reply draft |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Called it | Good call. What made you pick {color}? |
| 2 | Called it | You saw it early. {color} was behind until second {n}. |
| 3 | Guessed wrong | So close. {color} led almost to the end. |
| 4 | Rigged | No editing. The code runs the physics, and the result is what you see. |
| 5 | Rigged, with detail | Fair question. I run many random starts and post the close ones. I never change a result. |
| 6 | How is this made | Python code. It draws every frame and makes the sound. No game engine. |
| 7 | Is this AI | Not AI video. A physics program draws each frame. The code is written with an AI assistant. |
| 8 | What song is this | No song. Each hit plays a note, so the simulation makes its own music. |
| 9 | Use song X | I can't use other people's songs (copyright). The balls can play a new melody: calm or fast? |
| 10 | Mechanic idea, usable | Good idea. It is on the list. If we build it, we credit you. |
| 11 | Mechanic idea, not usable | Thank you. I can't use {brand or song}, but {close version} could work. Would you watch that? |
| 12 | Complaint: slow or long | You are right, {part} is slow. The next one is shorter. |
| 13 | Complaint: hard to see | Thank you. The next videos use shapes or numbers too, not only colors. |
| 14 | Other language | {Thank you in their language} 🙌 Which one did you pick? |
| 15 | Bot or spam | No reply, no heart. Report as spam, hide the user, add the repeated phrase to blocked words. |

Notes:
- 4 and 5 must stay true. Say "I post the close ones" only if runs are selected. If the generator exposes a seed, give it ("Seed 4172, the same result every time").
- 10: use it only when the idea really is on the list. 12 and 13: promise a fix only if the next video has it.
- 14: use the viewer's language only for short phrases we are sure of, otherwise simple English. Gracias / Obrigado / Merci / Danke / Terima kasih / Teşekkürler / Спасибо / شكرا / धन्यवाद / ありがとう.

## 3. Turning comments into content

1. Log each usable suggestion the day it arrives: date, handle, video, exact words, status (new, planned, built, or rejected with the reason).
2. Filter: visibly different from past mechanics, possible in our engine, no third-party brands, characters or songs, nothing cruel or unsafe.
3. Build it. Mark it on screen without words, for example a small speech-bubble icon [H], so "viewer idea" videos are recognizable in any language.
4. Credit in three places: the description ("Idea: @handle"), the pinned comment ("This was @handle's idea. What do we test next?") and a reply on the original comment ("Your idea is live. It is the newest Short on the channel.").
5. Same idea from several people: credit the first and say "first to suggest". Handle only, no avatars and no comment screenshots. Remove the credit if the viewer asks.

Cadence [H]: once suggestions exist, about one video in four or five is a viewer idea. A new channel at around 1,000 views per Short gets few comments, so until then the pinned question and polls are the source. Never invent "viewer requests".

Posts and polls. Whether a brand-new channel can publish posts and polls is not certain [U]: the requirement has changed more than once (subscriber thresholds at first; later, as far as we know, tied to feature access). Check on day 1 under Studio > Create > Create post. If it is missing, run the same votes in the pinned comment ("A or B? Reply with one letter") and check again weekly.
Cadence when available [H]: two posts a week, one poll and one result or behind-the-code post. Never more than one a day, and never a post that only says "new video".

Drafts ({ } = fill with true values only):
1. Poll: "What do we simulate next?" Options: Magnets / Gravity flips every 5 seconds / Balls split on every hit / The arena shrinks.
2. Poll: "Tomorrow: 4 colors, 1 exit, 100 balls each. Who wins?" Options: Red / Blue / Green / Yellow.
3. Result: "{x}% of you picked {color}. The winner was {color}. Were you right?" Image: the last frame.
4. Behind the code: "This video is {n} lines of Python. No game engine. What should the code do next?" Image: a debug frame or a few real lines.
5. Poll: "A bug this week: {what really happened} 😅 Do you want to see the failed runs?" Options: Yes / No.
6. Thank you: "{n} of you are here now. Thank you. You choose the rule for the next one." Options: 1,000 balls / Zero gravity / One ball, one tiny exit / Everything doubles.

## 4. On-video devices (no begging)

- **Question in the first second.** Frame 1 shows the stake in four words at most ("Which color wins?") plus the option chips. The title asks the same question. No intro, no logo. [H]
- **A visible stake.** A scoreboard, counter, timer, finish line or "alive" row shows who leads at every moment. Lead changes are the content, so post runs that have them.
- **An honest reveal.** The result is shown clearly, the winner is marked, and it stays on screen for about a second [H]. Never cut before the result, never use "wait for it" when nothing happens, never edit a run to look closer than it was. Choosing which run to post is fine; changing a result is not.
- **A loop.** The last frame resets into the first so a replay feels natural. The loop comes after the full reveal, never in place of it.
- **A wordless vote for the next video.** Two icons and "?" for one second at the end, in one video in three at most [H], so it does not become a template.
- **A season table** [H, optional]. Count wins per color across videos and post the table in pinned comments or posts. Viewers pick a side and come back. It must be exact.
- **Readable for everyone.** Color plus shape or number (color-blind viewers), marks large enough for small phones, and a stake that works with the sound off.
- **Episode number** in a corner ("#07"), so viewers can refer to a video in any language.
- Not used: "like if you were right", "subscribe to see the winner", "99% fail", countdowns to nothing, arrows pointing at the subscribe button.

## 5. Daily Studio reading (10 minutes)

Where: Studio > Content > Shorts > a video > Analytics, plus channel Analytics. Some numbers arrive a day or more late, so never judge a video on its first hours. Compare each Short with **our own median of the last 10 Shorts**, never with numbers from other channels: we have no verified external benchmarks.

| Metric | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Viewed versus swiped away | Whether frame 1 stops the swipe: hook, contrast, question. |
| Average percentage viewed, with the retention curve | Whether the middle holds and the loop works. It can pass 100% on a real loop. The curve shows the second where people leave. |
| Engaged views | Progress toward the 10 million goal. Since 2025 the public view count includes every start and replay; engaged views is the stricter count. YouTube said Partner Program eligibility stays on engaged views; confirm on the Earn page [U]. |
| Subscribers gained per video | Which videos grow the channel, not only collect views. |
| Comments per video, and what they say | Whether the prediction was clear and the reveal was trusted. |
| Top countries and viewer active hours, if shown | Posting time, and how much English text is safe. |

Decision rules, all [H]:
1. Too little data: under a few hundred feed impressions (our own floor, not a YouTube number) means no conclusion yet.
2. Viewed share below our median, percentage viewed normal: the opening is the problem. Change frame 1 (bigger stake, stronger contrast, shorter question) and keep the mechanic.
3. Viewed share fine, percentage viewed below our median: pacing. Find the drop on the retention curve, cut what comes before it, bring the first lead change earlier, shorten the video.
4. Both above our median: publish a sibling within 2 to 3 days, with the same kind of stake and a visibly different mechanic. Never a clone.
5. Many views, few subscribers per 1,000 engaged views: the video pleased but did not show what the channel is. Check that style, episode number and pinned question make the series obvious. Do not add a subscribe request.
6. Many "rigged" or "fake" comments: the reveal was unclear or looked too convenient. Show the stake more openly and answer with templates 4 and 5.
7. A weak video stays up. Do not delete it; write one line in the log on what to change.
8. Change one thing per video (length, opening, stake type, sound), so a difference has a known cause.
9. Weekly: sort by subscribers gained per 1,000 engaged views and by percentage viewed. The top two set next week's direction, the bottom two say what to drop.

Log per video: date, mechanic, length, opening, viewed share, percentage viewed, engaged views, subscribers, comments, one note.

## 6. Avoid: policy and trust risks

- **Misleading bait.** A title, first frame or pinned comment that promises something the video does not show; a faked or edited result; "wait for the end" with no end. Misleading metadata falls under YouTube's spam and deceptive-practices rules.
- **Fake giveaways.** Never announce a prize we will not deliver, and announce none in the first months. Real contests on YouTube need published rules and free entry, and must follow local law.
- **Sub-for-sub and bought engagement.** Sub-for-sub, bought views, subscribers or comments, engagement groups, bots, and second accounts commenting on our own videos are all fake engagement. Counts can be removed and the channel closed.
- **Repetitive mass replies.** The same sentence pasted under many comments, or promotional comments left on other channels' videos, is comment spam.
- **Deleting and re-uploading.** It throws away comments and data, and posting the same video again and again looks like the repetitive content that Partner Program review rejects. One exception [H]: a real technical fault (wrong file, broken sound) noticed within minutes; delete once and upload the fixed file.
- **Vote-begging** ("like = red, comment = blue"). Not clearly forbidden as far as we know [U], but it is begging and adds nothing. We skip it.
- **Wrong settings and tags.** A wrong audience setting, misleading hashtags, tags unrelated to the video.
- **Hearting or pinning** a comment that is hateful, sexual or a scam. A heart is an endorsement.
- **Asking for personal details** (age, school, city, contacts). Many viewers of this format may be young.
- **Third-party material.** Songs "because a viewer asked", other channels' clips, comment screenshots, brand names.
- **Claims we cannot prove.** "World's first", made-up counts, invented "you asked for this".

To verify once in Studio [U]: posts and polls for a new channel; which view count the Earn page uses; whether pinning and hearting can be done outside Studio and the app (to our knowledge the public Data API can post comments and replies but has no pin or heart call).
